## Part A — Frontier map

**[speculation]** There is a potentially publishable empirical frontier here: large exact-output equivalence mass in released residual-code text models, plus a replicated, instrument-specific localization of a severe continuation failure. There is no demonstrated better generator or general law of iterative generation yet.

**[source]** Plausible errors are not uniformly advocated by the field. GIDD explicitly benefits from uniform noise; corrective diffusion supervises visible mistakes; SAR changes supervision alongside student rollouts. These settings differ from retaining a wrong ancestor while predicting its canonical finer continuation. [von Rütte et al., 2025, ICML, arXiv:2503.04482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04482), [Zhang et al., 2025/2026, arXiv:2512.15596](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.15596), [Zhou et al., 2025, arXiv:2512.06421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.06421).

**[source]** Correct the brief's citation: arXiv:2605.26436 is withdrawn, with a successor that explicitly discusses inconsistent retained tokens and context contamination. It is evidence against presenting a unanimous “model-like mistakes must help” premise. [Yao, 2026, withdrawal notice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26436), [successor, arXiv:2604.18738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18738).

**[source]** Canonical versus marginal text probability is established; residual quantization and reconstruction/generation co-design are established too. I did not verify a prior report of your exact conditional decoder-fiber fraction in residual-VQ text. [Cao and Rimell, 2021, EMNLP, arXiv:2109.02550](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.161/), [Geh et al., 2024, EMNLP, arXiv:2408.08541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08541), [Pohl et al., 2025, arXiv:2510.20208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20208), [Lee et al., 2022, CVPR, arXiv:2203.01941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.01941), [Baade et al., 2026, arXiv:2610.20533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20533).

**[speculation]** The assumption your results challenge is conditional: matching the *appearance* of inference errors is sufficient to choose a good corruption channel while retaining the old labels and transition rule. They do not contradict proper denoising, useful on-policy distillation, or successful visual residual retargeting.

**[speculation]** Three framing corrections matter: the oracle-prefix curve does not attribute the NCM–AR gap; S0's weak neighbour probe does not establish weak general within-level dependence; E1's large fraction does not establish wasted compute or harmful gradients.

**[speculation]** Direct answer: the two narrowly stated measurements plausibly extend the public empirical frontier; a general explanation is unproved. The single most valuable added result would be a prospectively predicted *reversal* of the corruption effect across NCM and one editable denoising model, after controlling replacement marginals and compute, with a causal rescue. That would test a transferable principle rather than add another NCM ablation. My probability of achieving that strong result within three weeks is about 20%, rising to 35% with a well-gated larger programme.

## Part B — Divergent brainstorm

**[speculation]** Moves: **E** explain an anomaly; **I** invert an assumption; **X** import a mechanism; **Q** change the question; **L** push a limit; **P** combine findings. “Fresh” means distinct from the supplied inventory and the three earlier review lists, not novelty-cleared against all research. “Extension” explicitly builds on that inventory. This section deliberately makes no budget or feasibility selection.

1. **[speculation] Randomize context association — E, X, Q; anomalies 1, 3, 6; extension of donor controls.**
   Treat replacement generation as a statistical experiment: preserve code frequencies, masks and target counts, but randomize which example receives each plausible wrong code at L2–L5. This changes conditional association without replacing a non-uniform marginal by uniform noise.
   **Prediction:** the randomized arm rescues at least 25% of M2's excess scorer log loss, despite an unchanged replacement histogram; subjective probability **65%**. A positive result makes example-conditioned corruption, rather than marginal non-uniformity alone, a causal suspect; spatial coherence and displacement still need separate controls.

2. **[speculation] Cross corruption with what the transition can repair — E, I, Q; anomalies 1, 8, 16; fresh factorial.**
   Cross uniform/plausible noise with immutable-context prediction versus a transition that predicts and actually writes back corrections to that context. Include a loss-only repair head: extra supervision without writeback need not change the Bayes-optimal finer conditional.
   **Prediction:** plausible-minus-uniform damage is smaller with repair writeback than with an immutable ancestor, with an interaction of at least 0.1 nats/output-token on a controlled task; **55%**. This would challenge unconditional prescriptions to imitate inference noise across different transition operators.

3. **[speculation] Measure finite-horizon disturbance gain — E, X, P; anomalies 3, 6, 13, 16; extension of response maps.**
   Import control theory: a stable final state can coexist with large transient amplification. Perturb the same middle-level embedding direction, replay the suffix, and measure output change per unit perturbation rather than an equilibrium spectral radius.
   **Prediction:** the median downstream gain at L2–L5 is at least twice L1's, and larger for M2 suffixes on a shared bank; **65%**. This would turn the replay interaction into a directional, falsifiable dynamical account.

4. **[speculation] Recombine successful fine paths — E, X, P; anomalies 11, 12, 14; fresh measurement.**
   Import reliability theory: many valid configurations help independent sampling only if their set tolerates coordinate recombination. Decode mixtures of distinct successful suffixes for the same clamped prefix, using whole-level and within-level recombination separately.
   **Prediction:** at cut 8, successful-path recombination retains the exact output at least 20 percentage points more often than a matched unconditioned-path control; **65%**. This measures useful shape of the decoder's acceptance region, rather than merely counting alternatives.

5. **[speculation] Duplicate-label negative control — I, L, Q; anomaly 11; fresh theory/control.**
   Split each fine code into m identical decoder aliases and divide its probability equally. Output probabilities and samples are unchanged while canonical NLL rises by n log m for n aliased decisions.
   **Prediction:** the reported representation fraction can approach 1 with exactly zero output change; **98%** for a correct toy implementation. This exposes any argument that uses E1's fraction alone as evidence of wasted computation or a tokenizer-design defect.

6. **[speculation] Adversarial equivalent encodings — E, I; anomalies 9, 11; extension of fiber training.**
   Among verified suffixes producing the same reconstruction, choose the least likely target instead of the encoder's target, without changing the training text. Compare gradient directions and the ranking of checkpoints under these alternative path labels.
   **Prediction:** checkpoint code-NLL rankings can reverse while output-event probability is fixed; **60%**. This tests the robustness of model selection to an arbitrary within-output representative, without claiming a better generator.

7. **[speculation] Clean-row hysteresis — E, X, L; anomalies 4, 8; fresh.**
   Import phase-transition hysteresis: sweep clean-row share upward and downward with matched cumulative exposures. A learned regime boundary may retain memory of the route through training even after the current mixture matches.
   **Prediction:** at the same final clean fraction, the two paths differ by at least 2 Gen-PPL and by generated-context JS, beyond a reset-optimizer control; **45%**. If true, a static corruption channel is insufficient to explain continuation outcomes.

8. **[speculation] Sweep proposal temperature to both limits — E, L; anomalies 1–3; extension.**
   Interpolate a frozen proposal from nearly deterministic wrong neighbours to near-uniform wrong codes, keeping disagreement and clean share fixed. Measure retained-target compatibility and later amplification alongside temperature.
   **Prediction:** harm peaks at an intermediate conditional informativeness, rather than increasing monotonically with wrong-code probability; **65%**. This would replace the realistic/unrealistic binary with a measurable channel curve.

9. **[speculation] Make context earn trust — X, I, P; anomalies 4, 5, 7; extension of provenance routing.**
   Import adverse-selection screening: an ancestor is trusted only when two independently trained conditional predictions agree on an observable consequence, not because a learned flag declares it clean. Gate the context contribution by this certificate.
   **Prediction:** at matched accepted-context coverage, certified contexts have lower future canonical-target conflict than confidence-only selection; **35%**. This would make provenance operational when true corruption flags are unavailable.

10. **[speculation] Separate output-sensitive and output-insensitive updates — E, X; anomalies 8, 9, 11; extension of gradient protection.**
    Use finite decoder perturbations to estimate directions that preserve token margins, then downweight canonical fine-code gradients confined to those directions. A code-NLL improvement inside a flat decoder region should not dominate shared-parameter updates.
    **Prediction:** generated-context coarse divergence falls while fine canonical NLL improves less; **40%**. A quality gain would support harmful gradient allocation, which E1 alone cannot establish.

11. **[speculation] Invariance only over verified equivalent contexts — E, I, P; anomalies 1, 11, 14; fresh combination.**
    Train conditional predictions to agree on context pairs whose completed decoder outputs are verified identical, while retaining differences for pairs that change text. This avoids treating every plausible wrong code as harmless augmentation.
    **Prediction:** the invariance penalty is smaller for successful fiber edits than for distance-matched text-changing edits and improves generated-context robustness; **40%**. This supplies a testable boundary for latent augmentation in other generators.

12. **[speculation] Give early and late levels separate parameters — E, I; anomalies 6, 8, 9; extension.**
    Replace shared middle/fine updates with independent small adapters under the same parameter and compute budget. Canonical fine targets can otherwise alter the conditional policy responsible for plans.
    **Prediction:** removing late-to-early parameter interference rescues at least 25% of clean-row degradation with unchanged training examples; **50%**. A null would weaken optimization interference, without proving the corruption channel is correct.

13. **[speculation] An adversarial tokenizer–generator game — I, X; anomalies 11–14; fresh, high risk.**
    Let the encoder search for hard representations of a fixed text while the decoder must reconstruct and the generator must model an entire accepted class. The adversary exposes fragile canonical choices instead of rewarding reconstruction alone.
    **Prediction:** the learned representation has larger recombination tolerance at matched reconstruction accuracy; **15%**. Reviewers may dismiss instability, but a constrained adversary could reveal which tokenizers are generation-friendly in a way reconstruction loss cannot.

14. **[speculation] Render–reencode a generated state — I, X, P; anomalies 5, 11, 14; extension of encoder verification.**
    Use the encoder as a projection from a generated path to a canonical path for its own decoded text, then test whether subsequent conditional predictions become more coherent. Projection removes latent nuisance coordinates but can also erase useful uncertainty.
    **Prediction:** on a shared generated bank, projection reduces M2–M3 predictive divergence more than on a real bank, without improving the existing final text; **30%**. This isolates sensitivity to representation choice from immediate text quality.

15. **[speculation] Probe variable binding, not lexical inventory — E, Q; anomalies 10, 12, 13; extension of minimal-pair probes.**
    Hold bags of words fixed while swapping agent/patient roles, negation scope or repeated-name binding. Measure the earliest level that distinguishes these paired texts and whether that distinction survives corruption.
    **Prediction:** L2–L5 predicts the correct binding substantially above the bag-only baseline while L1 does not; **65%**. This could explain an order failure that lexical probes miss, and specify what a coarse representation should preserve.

16. **[speculation] Randomize nuisance coordinates during training — I, X; anomalies 9, 11; extension, high risk.**
    Apply verified within-fiber code transformations and train a generator equivariant to them, so canonical identity cannot serve as a shortcut. Ordinary global code-ID permutation is a negative control because it changes no geometry.
    **Prediction:** fine NLL becomes less predictive of held-out quality while output-event likelihood improves; **25%**. The risk is destroying information needed by later residual updates; the reason to try is to remove nuisance targets without forcing one new canonical path.

17. **[speculation] Fine levels as error-correcting syndromes — E, X, I; anomalies 11, 14, 16; fresh, high risk.**
    Reserve part of the tail for explicit consistency constraints on a coarse plan, allowing multiple code paths per output but making incorrect combinations detectable. These are syndromes for generated-state errors, not just more residual detail.
    **Prediction:** at the same tail length and reconstruction error, independent-code perturbations preserve output at least twice as often as in a matched residual tokenizer; **20%**. Dismissing redundant bits as waste misses their potential coding gain.

18. **[speculation] Generate fine anchors before the coarse bag — I, X; anomalies 10, 13, 15; fresh, high risk.**
    Generate a sparse set of grammatical or entity-binding anchors, infer the coarse plan conditional on them, then fill remaining details. Coarse-to-fine frequency order need not be the best order for a language constraint graph.
    **Prediction:** a fixed anchor budget reduces role-binding errors more than the same number of coarse bag codes; **20%**. It may fail because anchors are hard to predict; it could work because a few relations remove much larger plan ambiguity.

19. **[speculation] Infinitely many infinitesimal levels — I, X, L; anomalies 6, 13, 16; fresh theory question.**
    At fixed total representation rate and training compute, shrink each residual step while increasing depth. Ask whether refinement approaches a contractive flow or merely increases sequential error exposure.
    **Prediction:** downstream disturbance gain initially falls with smaller steps, then latency and accumulated conditional error dominate; **30%**. This tests whether level count is numerical step size or an information bottleneck; it predicts no automatic continuous-limit advantage.

20. **[speculation] The zero-information corruption limit — X, L, Q; anomalies 1, 7, 8; fresh analytic control.**
    Take context corruption to an example-independent distribution with no clean component. The population optimum for a context-only target becomes its marginal; sample quality then depends on how the generator uses that conditional at inference.
    **Prediction:** a finite exact-distribution toy matches that conditional-collapse limit and shows target conflict can increase even when corruption is easy to recognize; **85%**. This would falsify any theory claiming detector AUC alone guarantees harmless corruption.

21. **[speculation] Damp only high-gain directions — E, X; anomalies 3, 6, 16; extension of relaxation.**
    Import preconditioning: shrink a sampled residual's component along directions with large measured terminal influence, rather than lowering temperature everywhere. Preserve directions that change detail without destabilizing the plan.
    **Prediction:** direction-selective damping improves order at the same diversity more than scalar damping; **40%**. This would connect a response measurement to a sampler, but it needs a comparator with the same added decoder work.

22. **[speculation] An exact small-world benchmark — Q, X, I; anomalies 1, 7, 11; fresh measurement/theory.**
    Construct a finite language with binding and exact output fibers, allowing enumeration of clean and corrupted joints and exact generation distributions. Train identical small models under immutable and repairable context transitions.
    **Prediction:** one can change canonical NLL without changing output KL, and change output KL without a large canonical-NLL change; **65%** for both under matched finite capacity. This makes a universal-sounding mechanism falsifiable without trusting a neural text scorer.

23. **[speculation] Cross-seed replay compatibility — E, Q, P; anomalies 5, 6, 11; fresh instrument control.**
    Cross every M2 seed's prefix with every M3 seed's suffix, and also cross within-arm seeds. A replay penalty may reflect co-adaptation beyond a single level range's intrinsic damage.
    **Prediction:** M2's prefix remains harmful across suffix seeds, while same-arm cross-seed penalties are below 10% of the M2–M3 contrast; **70%**. Failure would narrow localization to specific checkpoint pairings and change the causal language of the paper.

24. **[speculation] Change fiber topology at fixed mass — X, Q, P; anomalies 11, 14, 15; fresh, high risk.**
    In an exactly enumerable decoder, hold canonical probability, number of valid paths and total valid mass fixed, but arrange the valid set as a rectangle or disconnected correlated islands. Compare factorized and joint perturbations.
    **Prediction:** independent perturbation tolerance differs by at least 2× despite identical E1 statistics; **25%** for a nontrivial matched construction carrying over to a learned tokenizer. The construction may seem artificial, but it identifies what E1 omits and gives a precise tokenizer-design target.

25. **[speculation] Rare contexts carry most damage — E, X, Q; anomalies 3, 5, 6, 9; fresh.**
    Import tail-risk analysis: average real-context CE can hide a small generated-state subset with large terminal influence. Predefine context bins using a separate bank and measure excess duplicate risk or scorer loss after suffix replay.
    **Prediction:** the worst 10% of bank prefixes explain at least half the excess terminal loss, more than their share of clean NLL; **60%**. This could replace mean teacher-forced NLL with a diagnostic that matters to actual rollout reliability.

26. **[speculation] A mutation error threshold — E, X, L; anomalies 1, 3, 4; fresh.**
    Import evolutionary error thresholds: coherent plans persist until perturbations cross a boundary, after which local order collapses and extra wrong slots do little. Sweep concentration while holding code disagreement fixed.
    **Prediction:** an abrupt change in duplicate rate and plan-consistency statistics fits better than a linear dose response on an untouched sweep; **35%**. A smooth response kills the threshold story rather than merely moving its fitted location.

27. **[speculation] Audit restriction/prolongation cancellation — E, X; anomalies 6, 12, 14; extension of lifting and multigrid.**
    Measure whether downsampling the next residual undoing an upsampled coarse perturbation actually cancels it. Learned interpolation and convolution may create directions that finer codebooks cannot correct.
    **Prediction:** large restriction/prolongation mismatch predicts terminal damage beyond embedding distance, with positive held-out partial correlation; **45%**. This would motivate a specific multiscale consistency constraint instead of generic tokenizer regularization.

28. **[speculation] Maintain a posterior over compatible futures — I, X, P; anomalies 1, 11, 13; extension of reassignment/fiber learning.**
    When an ancestor changes, carry multiple compatible future paths instead of forcing the original canonical suffix. Approximate a posterior over complete paths, preserving cross-position and cross-level compatibility.
    **Prediction:** joint-path targets rescue more damage than independently softened per-position labels at matched target entropy; **35%**. This is a different hypothesis from the already failed soft-label recipe, but posterior approximation could still fail.

29. **[speculation] Send scorer-based selection to infinity — Q, L; anomalies 9, 10; fresh project stress test.**
    Increase best-of-n selection under GPT-2 without changing the generator; track another scorer, sample uniqueness, lexical coverage and sequence-level diversity. An arbitrarily good selected Gen-PPL may reward a narrow subset of stories.
    **Prediction:** the apparent improvement continues after independent coverage metrics deteriorate; **80%**. This would demonstrate a failure of the project's own selection practice if it ever uses raw Gen-PPL alone; it would not refute all scorer-based evaluation.

30. **[speculation] Predict transfer before intervention — Q, P, X; anomalies 1, 6, 11, 16; fresh programme.**
    Treat a generator as a context channel, a target rule, a decoder acceptance region and a finite-horizon transition. Register corruption-effect signs on a held-out family from these measurements before training it.
    **Prediction:** the four quantities predict the sign of at least five of six new intervention contrasts, beating disagreement count and detector AUC; **35%**. A replicated result would matter to masked LMs and next-scale image models even if NCM quality never improves.

### Direction 1 — Does conditional association make plausible noise harmful? (ideas 1, 3, 23, 25)

**[speculation] Core.** Test a randomized association between the same multiset of plausible wrong replacements and the training examples retaining their canonical finer targets. Start at the implicated middle levels; L1 reassignment remains killed by its registered gate, and is not revived by changing its threshold.

**[speculation] Derivation.** Write A for a canonical context and Y for the finer target. Training a context-only predictor under channel C learns

\[
p_C(y\mid u)=\frac{\sum_a p(a,y)C(u\mid a,y)}{\sum_{a,y'}p(a,y')C(u\mid a,y')}.
\]

**[speculation]** The y argument permits a corruption rule whose canonical exclusion or proposal conditioning depends on the original example. Similar Hamming damage or the same marginal p(u) does not imply the same p_C(y|u). A plausible replacement can preserve misleading evidence about Y; uniform replacement can be easier for the network to discount. Neither statement establishes the sign of sample-quality change: generation also changes the occupancy of U and amplifies selected conditional errors. The successful root-channel prediction demonstrates why the channel equation must be paired with downstream replay.

**[speculation] Intervention.** Cache candidate replacements from the frozen proposer on a training-only bank. At L2–L5, form constrained permutations over masked slots within each level and position, preserving replacement counts, masks, data order and target counts and forbidding replacement by the receiving example's canonical code. Keep the other levels' procedure fixed. Count displaced slots and report the fraction that can participate; require at least 80% before interpreting a null. The direct control runs the same cache and preprocessing but retains the original association. Both arms pay the proposal/cache cost.

**[speculation]** Call this arm D and the unpermuted arm P. The estimand is the effect of randomized example association at fixed empirical replacement marginals. Slotwise permutation also removes example-level coherence; this experiment does not uniquely separate semantic incompatibility, within-row coherence and embedding displacement. Record those quantities, then use distance-stratified permutations as a prespecified secondary control only if coverage remains adequate. A donor from another row is not automatically a clean semantic control.

**[speculation] Kill, at most 5 CU.** First use CPU invariants for histograms, exclusion, manifest refusal and exact resume, plus 128 fixed validation contexts to verify that conditional alignment actually changes. Then run P, D and a matched U reference for 175 continuation steps at seed 0, with 25% clean rows. Allow about 2.7 CU for P/D, 0.8 for U, 0.4 for cache construction and 1 for fp32 evaluation; stop at 5. The P pilot must produce at least 0.15 nats/token excess scorer loss over U at that same step; otherwise the short pilot is inconclusive. Never substitute a 600-step reference. If actual preprocessing or evaluation cannot fit the cap, shorten all three arms prospectively or declare the pilot underpowered.

**[speculation] Prediction.** Let L be the registered mean scorer log loss and U the matched uniform reference. Predict r = (L_P − L_D)/(L_P − L_U) ≥ 0.25 under both scorers, with D's duplicates no worse than P's, MAUVE not lower by >0.02, and unigram entropy not lower than U's by >0.05. Kill expansion if r ≤0.10, if the rescue requires entropy collapse, or if randomization coverage is inadequate. Intermediate intervals buy no automatic confirmation. Probability of substantive rescue conditional on a valid pilot: **65%**; probability of identifying semantic conflict uniquely from this test: **below 30%**.

**[speculation] Claim and replication.** Target criterion (c), narrowed to a causal property of the middle-level training channel. Three new paired seeds 4–6, P/D at 600 steps, cost about 27.6 CU before shared final evaluation; seed 0 stays a pilot. Repeat the replay instrument and cross prefixes with other suffix seeds. Report per-seed effects, seed SD and paired intervals; retain the gain-over-2×-pooled-SD rule as an additional requirement. Predict ≥25% rescue of the log-loss contrast, not recovery of released quality. Rescue from P is not criterion (a).

**[speculation] Objection and answer.** “You destroyed conditional coherence and changed embedding damage, so this is still not a plausibility result.” Correct: the primary claim is about association at fixed marginals. Displacement/coherence controls determine whether it can be narrowed further. Do not rename the estimand after seeing the result.

**[speculation] Existing assets and raised budget.** W2C supplies effect sizes and P/U checkpoints; W2M-GR supplies cuts and replay code; generated banks support tail-risk and cross-seed controls; the Sudoku analogue supplies a non-collapse boundary. Extra funding is better used on a prospectively predicted second-family interaction than on six longer damaged NCM arms. ### Direction 2 — Is redundancy useful tolerance or merely path multiplicity? (ideas 4, 5, 24)

**[speculation] Core.** Measure whether independent perturbations stay inside the decoder's set of accepted paths. E1 measures probability mass over that set; it does not measure its geometry or the effect of removing its dimensions.

**[speculation] Derivation.** For fixed prefix a, canonical suffix c*, and y = D(a,c*), define F_y(a) = {z : D(a,z)=y}. At the registered temperature and ancestral sampler,

\[
R=N+\log\rho=-\log Q(c^*\mid D(a,C)=y,a),\qquad
\rho=Q(F_y(a)\mid a).
\]

**[speculation]** This is posterior surprisal of the chosen path, not the entropy of all valid paths and not a bound on removable computation. Equal-size valid sets behave differently: {00,01} is closed under coordinate recombination; {00,11} is not, and independent fair-coordinate draws retain the latter event only half the time. Conversely, cloning each label m times adds n log m to canonical surprisal without changing decoded outputs. These controls show why neither fiber cardinality nor E1's fraction alone decides tokenizer quality.

**[speculation] Eval-only test, capped at 2 CU.** Register eight canonical-prefix items per released domain, plus four generated-prefix items per domain, before inspecting outcomes. At cut 8, sample at most 256 suffixes/item, save complete codes, and retain every hit count, including zeros. For generated prefixes, define the reference y with an independent reference suffix before drawing the test bank. On items with at least eight distinct successful suffixes, recombine across whole levels and across positions within one level. Keep a frozen-full-path decoder test separate from a test that ancestrally resamples later levels: only the latter includes the generator's corrective response.

**[speculation]** Measure exact-output retention after single-site and multi-site edits from successful alternatives, whole-level and within-level recombination, and zeroing the same embedding contributions. Repeat operations on an unconditioned suffix bank at the same prefix. Report coverage and uncertainty over every registered item; sparse-hit items remain unresolved and cannot disappear from the conclusion. Exact reconstruction equality is primary; Hamming distance and decoder margins are secondary. These are counterfactual decoder probes, not estimators of ancestral text likelihood.

**[speculation] Prediction and kill.** Predict a ≥20-point retention advantage for successful-bank recombinations over the unconditioned-bank control, in at least two domains, and worse retention after erasing the same contributions. Probability **65%**. Kill the strong “broad acceptance regions tolerate independent edits” account if within-level recombination retention is ≤10% on adequately sampled items or single-site edits from successful alternatives frequently fail. Insufficient successes within the cap are inconclusive, with coverage reported. Do not spend unbounded compute on only easy items.

**[speculation] What it separates.** High edit/recombination retention with poor erasure retention supports useful tolerance in the frozen system: alternate configurations perform necessary work. Low retention despite substantial rho suggests compensating combinations or cross-level dependencies. High retention plus many removable contributions motivates a compressed-tail pilot. None establishes how a retrained injective tokenizer would behave. No eval-only measurement can identify that training counterfactual; the literal “which is right?” question needs this qualification.

**[speculation] Replication and ambition.** Use the three released domains as replication for a frozen-model property. Add independently selected item batches with a fixed estimator and coverage rule, about 4–6 CU if the pilot is informative; this expansion would substitute for another optional allocation. Keep generated-prefix behavior separate from canonical-prefix E1. A theorem plus enumerable toy can hold valid-set mass and canonical mass fixed while varying recombination geometry. Probability of a defensible new frozen-model statement **60%**; probability of an immediate ≥25% text latency improvement **15%**.

**[speculation] Objection and answer.** “Conditioning on successful paths manufactures a robust-looking sample.” The unconditional bank, one-level controls, fixed perturbation densities and coverage report expose selection. The conclusion concerns conditional geometry; it does not say that successes are frequent under arbitrary prefixes or that their probability is high across all items.

**[speculation] Existing assets and raised budget.** Reuse E1's sampler, equality definition and interval machinery, released decoders and W2M's generated-prefix banks. Existing E1 hit-count JSONs cannot reconstruct suffix samples, so path collection is real work. Fund redesign only after identifying a compression operation preserving acceptance tolerance. Tokenizer redesign is not the default T1/T2 purchase.

### Direction 3 — Repairable context versus retained misinformation (ideas 2, 20, 22, 30)

**[speculation] Core.** Ask when a trained transition can remove misleading context before using it again. Test the transition explicitly, including an erasure baseline, rather than interpreting plausible-noise failure as a failure of on-policy learning in general.

**[speculation] Derivation and negative control.** Adding a loss for the corrupted ancestor while retaining the same inputs and sufficiently flexible heads does not alter the population-optimal p_C(Y|U) for a finer target. “Other methods reconstruct the corrupted token, therefore their finer conditional is automatically correct” is incomplete. A repair method must change subsequent states, the supervision distribution, or both. Writeback, remasking and a posterior-consistent chain are different mechanisms; an exact finite-state task can distinguish them without scorer ambiguity.

**[speculation] CPU kill first.** In an enumerable binding task, fix the clean joint and corruption channel. Compare immutable context, an ancestor-prediction loss without writeback, and correction with writeback; match evaluated transition counts and expose exact output KL. Predict that a loss-only head leaves the finer Bayes optimum unchanged, while writeback reduces plausible-minus-uniform output damage. A counterexample to the latter kills the universal sign law. SUNDAE's chain denoising and SAR's changed supervision are prior art.

**[speculation] NCM kill, at most 5 CU.** Candidate E0 uses no clean rows and replaces masked L2–L5 code embedding contributions by a fixed zero vector before existing upsampling/convolution. Other levels keep uniform corruption; rates, targets, data order, parameters and sampling depth stay fixed. Control U0 uses the original uniform-corruption recipe everywhere. Run a matched 400-step pair: about 3.6 CU training and ≤1.4 CU fp32 evaluation. This is a specific middle-level erasure/dropout ablation, not a new generic denoising architecture or a revived constant-flag arm.

**[speculation] Method gate.** Predict E0 improves both scorers and gains ≥1 GPT-2 Gen-PPL over U0 at the same step, without entropy dropping >0.05, MAUVE dropping >0.02 or increased duplicates. Probability **25%**; a successful pilot might gain 1–2 Gen-PPL. Kill if it loses by >0.5 or gains only through a diversity trade-off; 0–1 improvement buys no large purchase. If triggered, E0/U0 at 600 steps on three fresh seeds cost about 16.2 CU before shared evaluation. It must beat the no-clean control, not merely recover U25's explained drift.

**[speculation] Limits and claim.** E0 changes displacement and informativeness along with recognizability. A positive result is a method ablation; Direction 1 and the exact toy must earn its explanation. Target (c) first. A TinyStories-only gain does not satisfy (a)'s full-suite bar; there is no matched AR comparison for (b). A sharper optional criterion **(e)** would require a prospectively predicted corruption-by-transition sign interaction in two families, three fresh training seeds per causal contrast, complete quality/diversity reporting, and a mechanism-specific rescue. This is a proposed criterion requiring a user decision, not an amendment made here.

**[speculation] Plan, objection and assets.** The strongest objection is “ordinary erasure is known, and you changed several channel properties.” Agree about method novelty: the proposed contribution is a measured boundary and prospective transfer prediction. ### The redundancy question, and what redesign would cost

**[speculation] Current answer.** Fine-level multiplicity and useful error tolerance can coexist. E1 establishes the former. Decoder robustness and the necessity of the final levels make the latter plausible, but do not prove it. What predicts the outcome is acceptance-region mass under the actual product/ancestral proposal, its tolerance to local independent edits, and the remaining levels' ability to correct those edits. An injective representation can eliminate posterior path surprisal while making factorized errors much more costly; a redundant representation can also hide useless aliases. The 2-CU Direction-2 test distinguishes these local cases without pretending to answer the retraining counterfactual.

**[speculation] Design implication.** Preserve broad, locally tolerant decoder regions around useful outputs while removing aliases that add no tolerance. Optimize reconstruction, generation under perturbed factorized codes, and total training/inference cost jointly. An objective could maximize exact reconstruction plus log survival under a registered independent code-perturbation distribution, subject to a rate/length budget. Compare it with a reconstruction-only tokenizer and an alias-inflation negative control. This is more specific than simply minimizing canonical NLL or demanding injectivity.

**[speculation] NFE arithmetic.** Reducing 12 counted levels to 8 gives 0.667× under the paper's convention. With the repository's 11 actual generator forwards, eliminating three or four passes gives 8/11 =0.727× or 7/11 =0.636×. Neither establishes the same latency ratio: later passes contain more positions, and any learned fill/decoder/verification work must be counted. Truncating the existing tail is already contradicted by the supplied reconstruction evidence; redesign would be a different experiment.

**[source] Tokenizer training setting.** NCM Table 5 gives the TinyStories tokenizer as 36.43M parameters, width 384, no encoder transformer blocks, one decoder block, 30,000 updates and batch 4096. The separate level-count ablation trains tokenizers for 40k steps. These settings should not be conflated. [Kumbong, Khare et al., 2026, arXiv:2610.14162, Tables 5 and F.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14162v1).

**[speculation] Cost estimate, not calibration.** For the Table-5 setting, `src/ncmr/flops.py`'s tokenizer forward proxy, multiplied by three for training, gives about **1.72 EFLOP** over 122.88M windows. The crude 6NT rule using all 36.43M parameters and 15.73B tokens gives **3.44 EFLOP**; it overcounts some embeddings and ignores important quantizer/kernel details. **[speculation] Paper-scale redesign.** One new tokenizer plus six 3.5-EFLOP generator runs needs approximately **1104–1443 CU including allowance**, even before the 77-CU T0 programme.
