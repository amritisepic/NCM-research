# W2C + W2M-GR: scoped mechanistic result (user decision 2026-10-28, R068: reported as audited, not as criterion (c))

Basis: W2C stage 1 (R062), deep audit 12 (R063, `docs/reviews/codex_audit12.md`), W2M-GR (R065), deep audit 14
(R067, `docs/reviews/codex_audit14.md`, triage `docs/reviews/triage_audit14_2026-10-28.md`). The brief requires the user to hear of a result believed to meet the success
bar before more compute is spent on it (done 2026-10-28), and a claim audit before any claim (audit 14).

## Claim
Wording after deep audit 14 (R067; it supersedes R065's wording).

**Generation damage (W2C).** Under one continuation protocol for the released TinyStories NCM (600 steps, 25% clean
rows, W1's per-level corruption rates, one mask stream), replacing masked codes with wrong codes drawn from the frozen
released model given the canonical coarser levels caused additional generation degradation, relative to uniform-wrong
replacement in the same slots. Across three training seeds:
- M2 - M3 Gen-PPL was +18.9 under GPT-2 large (each seed at least +18.36; pooled seed SD 0.56).
- It was +29.8 under TinyStories-33M.
- MAUVE was -0.41, and adjacent BPE duplicates +12.3 per thousand pairs.
- Canonical teacher-forced NLL was higher by only 4.0 nats/window (0.53%), positive in every seed.

**Crossed replay (W2M-GR).** Crossed replay of whole level ranges reproduced a conditional response at the same three
seeds. Shares are of the pure-model contrast in GPT-2 large mean log loss of the decoded text:
- M2's levels 1-5 rendered by M3's levels 6-11 carry 52.7%.
- M2's level 1 alone, with M3's levels 2-11, carries 1.6%.
- M2's levels 6-11 on M3's levels 1-5 carry 5.3%.
- The prefix/suffix interaction is +0.444 and +0.420 at cuts 4 and 6.

Every seed is within about 0.03 of exploratory seed 0, and TinyStories-33M agrees.

What this establishes is a replicated, intervention-level finding about which level ranges carry a training-induced
generation failure that canonical NLL barely registers. It does not establish what those levels represent.

## Definitions the claim depends on
- **Protocol.** Continuation training of the released TinyStories NCM generator for 600 steps, with 25% of rows clean,
  W1's per-level corruption rates and one shared mask stream. Within a seed the two arms see identical batches, clean
  rows and masked slots. Executed traces in audit 12 show this; equal disagreement rates alone would not.
- **M2 ("plausible-wrong").** At each masked slot, a wrong code drawn at T = 1 from the frozen released model given
  the canonical coarser levels, with the canonical code excluded. The root is a draw from the released unigram, with
  the canonical root excluded. Not on-policy, no typicality filter.
- **M3 ("uniform-wrong").** A uniform draw over codes other than the canonical one, in the same slots.
- **Crossed replay (W2M-GR).** A cell (prefix P, suffix S, cut c) samples levels 1..c-1 with checkpoint P and levels
  c..11 with checkpoint S, ancestrally at T = 1, with common random numbers. There are 8 groups x 256 samples per cell,
  scored by GPT-2 large (TinyStories-33M beside), on an L4.
  - pi_c is the share of the M2 - M3 log-loss contrast carried by an M2 prefix with an M3 suffix.
  - sigma_c is the share carried by an M3 prefix with an M2 suffix.
  - interaction = 1 - pi_c - sigma_c.

Conditional contrasts the shares imply (seed means, GPT-2 large; audit 14):
- Switching levels 2-5 from M3 to M2 moves 0.511 of the contrast given M2's level 1 and M3's levels 6-11. It moves 0.828

- M2's level 1 moves 0.016 on an M3 continuation and 0.119 on an M2 continuation.

The shares are therefore not an allocation of the damage to levels.

## What it does not say
1. **Comparative only.** M3 is itself ~5.5 Gen-PPL above the released model (21.68 vs 16.16). The 25% clean rows
   cause that gap (R059). The claim is M2 against M3 under one protocol, not M2 against the released model.
2. **Plausibility is not shown to be the unique cause.**
   - No temperature-, frequency- or length-matched control exists.
   - No marginal-preserving control exists: the same plausible wrong codes, reassigned to other examples.
   - "Wrong codes drawn from the frozen released model" damage more than uniform-wrong codes. Whether any
     example-conditioned non-uniform wrong distribution would do the same is open.
3. **Not equal compute.** M2's training adds one proposal forward per step.
4. **One instrument for the localization.** The levels are replayed in whole ranges at cuts 2, 4 and 6. No single
   level and no cut at 3 or 5 was replayed. The shares are of a scorer's log loss of decoded text, and they are not
   additive (interaction ~0.42). A same-arm cross-seed replay was not run, so co-adaptation within one checkpoint is
   not excluded as part of the interaction. Concretely:
- Roots (released-unigram draws) and Gumbel noise are shared across cells.
- No single level and no cut at 3 or 5 was replayed, so "levels 2-5" means the block taken together, under the
  surrounding samplers. It does not mean "the damage forms there".
- An M3 suffix on an M2 prefix measures M3's response to a foreign-prefix distribution as well as the prefix itself.
- 0.53 x 18.9 is not an attributable Gen-PPL increment: the two halves use different units and different sample sets.
- The (i) and (iii) bootstrap intervals are transcribed from the runs, not recomputed: the per-window arrays are in the
  runs' Drive cache, not in the repository.
- W2C did not record its checkpoint hashes, so the identity of the replayed checkpoints rests on names and matching
  metrics (pure cells within 3 SE of W2C).
5. **One model, one domain, one protocol.** It is not about other domains, from-scratch training, other corruption
   rates or on-policy corruption. It is not an explanation of the NCM-AR gap.
6. **Scorer revisions and MAUVE features** are not pinned in the run manifests (audit 12, W8).

## Prior art (checked 2026-10-28, abstract pages)
- **Scheduled sampling**
  - Bengio et al. 2015 (arXiv 1506.03099) introduced it.
  - Huszár 2015 (arXiv 1511.05101) shows its objective is improper and its learning inconsistent. This is the closest
    conceptual account of why training on model-like inputs with the original targets can mislead.
- **Next-scale generation**
  - VAR (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2404.02905) established it.
  - SAR (Zhou et al. 2025, arXiv 2512.06421) studies train/test context mismatch and student forcing in scale-wise
    image generation. It does not report this crossed-replay localization or a text result.
- **Discrete diffusion with corrupted context**
  - GIDD (von Rütte et al., ICML 2025, arXiv 2503.04482) benefits from mixing uniform noise with masking.
  - Corrective diffusion LMs (Zhang et al., arXiv 2512.15596) supervise visible mistakes.
  - SUNDAE (Savinov et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv 2112.06749) denoises its own unrolled samples.
  - "Remask, Don't Replace" (Yao 2026, arXiv 2604.18738) notes that retained inconsistent tokens become context for
    later predictions. It is training-free and inference-only. It supersedes the withdrawn arXiv 2605.26436, which
    should not be cited.

What is new here: a same-slot, wrong-only, fresh-seed contrast in a residual-code text generator, its dissociation
from canonical NLL, and its replicated location in the hierarchy, measured by crossed replay. Two literature checks are
not a novelty clearance.

## Decision for the user
**The criterion question.** Brief (c) asks for "a replicated mechanistic finding about what the levels encode".
Deep audit 14 does not certify this result as (c) on that reading, and I agree. The result locates a failure in the
hierarchy; it does not show what the levels represent. Counting it would be your interpretive decision about the
success bar, not a stronger result.

Options:
1. **Recommended.** Claim C-E1 (`docs/claim_e1_2026-10-28.md`) as criterion (c), scoped. It speaks directly to what
   levels 8 and finer encode: mostly a choice among code paths with the same decoded output. Report W2C + W2M-GR as
   an audited, scoped mechanistic result alongside it, not as (c). Tradeoff: C-E1 has thin text margins on item
   resampling (limit 4 of that draft).
2. Count W2C + W2M-GR as (c) as well, under a broader reading: "a replicated mechanistic finding about the levels'
   roles in generation". Tradeoff: this changes how the success bar is read, and a hostile reader will say so.
3. Hold W2C + W2M-GR until two cheap tests close the open points:
   - cross-seed and single-level replay (~4-5 CU, eval-only, existing checkpoints);
   - the marginal-preserving association kill (<= 6 CU).
   Both are proposed in `docs/plan_2026-10-27.md` section 11. Tradeoff: about 10 CU and a week of build and review
   first. A positive result would narrow the alternatives, but it still would not show what the levels encode.

**Answer (user, 2026-10-28; R068):** option 1. C-E1 is claimed as (c); this result is reported as scoped above.
