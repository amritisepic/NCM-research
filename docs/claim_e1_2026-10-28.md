# E1: proposed criterion-(c) claim (draft for the user, 2026-10-28; not claimed)

Basis: E1 + E1a (R054), E1b (R060), deep audit 11 (R061, `docs/reviews/codex_audit11.md`). The brief requires the
user to hear of a result believed to meet the success bar before more compute is spent on it.

## Claim
On the registered items of each of the three released NCM models (TinyStories, WMT14, Sudoku-hard), given the
canonical codes of levels 0-7, at least 80% of the canonical code NLL of levels 8 and finer (N_8) is spent choosing
among code paths that decode to the same output. Lower 95% confidence bounds on mean R_8 / mean N_8, one per model,
covering Monte Carlo error only: TinyStories 0.894 (64 val windows; 0.888 on the 61 the pilot did not touch), WMT14
0.859 (128 newstest2013 pairs), Sudoku-hard 0.993 (128 puzzles). This is E1's registered prediction 1, read with
R044's replication unit (three domains). TinyStories and WMT14 are read after E1b, a registered continuation of the
same Monte Carlo streams. That is valid because the confidence sequences are anytime-valid: no new error budget is
needed, even though E1b was registered after E1's result. Deep audit 11 found no defect in the reading, and every
number recomputes independently.

## What it does not say
1. **Fine levels only.** N_8 is 46% (TinyStories), 46% (WMT14) and 74% (Sudoku) of the total canonical code NLL. As
   a share of the total, the lower bounds are 0.41 / 0.39 / 0.73. Coarser cuts (5-7) rarely hit and were not read.
2. **E1a's stronger version holds only in Sudoku.** That version needs a lower bound above 0.90 and at least 80% of
   items with a T_8 interval of at most 2 nats. TinyStories reaches 0.888 and WMT14 0.859.
3. **Not generation, not quality.** E1 measures fibers (the sets of code paths that decode to the same output) at
   canonical, teacher-forced coarse prefixes. It is not the text likelihood of free generation, and it does not
   explain generation quality. Prediction 2 was unreadable. Descriptively, N_8 and U_8 order the models exactly
   opposite to their quality in both text domains.
4. **On the registered items.** The bounds cover Monte Carlo error on fixed items, not the choice of items. As an
   exploratory check, I resampled items with replacement (20,000 draws, R061). The 5% quantile of the lower bound is
   0.82 for TinyStories (0.81 on the untouched windows), 0.80 for WMT14 and 0.99 for Sudoku. So as a statement about
   all windows, the text margins over 0.8 are thin. The censored items carry most of the gap: 5 TinyStories windows
   and 9 WMT14 pairs have no hit in 32,768 samples and enter the bound at R = 0. The items are the first registered
   ones, not a random sample, and WMT14's pairs are consecutive news sentences. If anything, the resampling therefore
   understates item uncertainty.
5. **"Same output"** is the decoder's reconstruction of the canonical codes. It equals the data in 63/64, 100/128 and
   128/128 items. WMT14 compares tokens up to the first EOS with pads dropped, so paths that differ only after the EOS
   count as the same output.
6. **One checkpoint per domain** (the released models). "Replicated" means across three domains (R044), not across
   seeds or training runs. Each bound is 95% for its own model. The three are not simultaneous, though the
   conjunction "all three are at least 0.8" is a valid 0.05-level test (intersection-union).
7. **Prior art.** Marginalizing over alternative representations of the same text is not new. Cao and Rimell (EMNLP
   2021, arXiv:2109.02550) study marginal likelihood over subword tokenizations in pretrained language models. What
   is new here is the measurement inside NCM's learned multi-level code, with anytime-valid bounds, and the size of
   the effect: on average at least 361 nats per TinyStories window are path choice.

## Decision for the user
Claim criterion (c) on this basis with the scope above, or hold the claim. A population-level statement would need
new sampling on more items, which means compute and is the user's call. Recommendation: claim it as scoped, on fixed
items, with limit 4 stated in the claim itself.
